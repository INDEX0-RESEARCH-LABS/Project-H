# 04 — Customer-Investor Pitch

**Reader:** a diagnostic-chain promoter, clinic owner, hospital administrator, or angel who already operates in Indian healthcare
**Ask:** ₹25 lakh for 10–12% at a ₹2.0–2.5 crore post-money valuation
**When:** month 12+, and **only after one paid pilot has renewed.** Not before. §9 explains what happens if you send it earlier.
**Format:** 12 slides, plus a 2-page memo, plus the pilot report as an appendix.

---

## Why this document exists, and why it is not a VC deck

The person reading this is not trying to find a 100× outcome. They already own clinics or labs, they already see the problem, and they are asking a different question:

> **"Will this make my existing business more money, and will I get my ₹25 lakh back?"**

A venture deck answers "how big can this get". This one answers "how does this pay me back, and what happens if it doesn't". Those are different documents, and confusing them is why most healthtech pitches to clinic owners fail.

### 1.1 What this investor gets that a VC does not

| | VC | Customer-investor |
|---|---|---|
| Wants | 10× in 7 years, or a write-off | Their money back plus a return, and a better clinic |
| Timeframe | Fund life, 7–10 years | Flexible; often indefinite |
| Exit | Required | Optional |
| Dividends | Rarely | Acceptable, sometimes preferred |
| Board control | Wants it | Usually does not want the work |
| Value beyond money | Network, hiring | **Distribution — they can make your next 10 sales calls** |
| What scares them | A small market | Losing money and looking foolish to their peers |

**Your pitch must be built for the right-hand column.** The distribution point is the most important line in this document: this investor can introduce you to every lab owner in their network, which is worth more than the ₹25 lakh.

## 2. The 12 slides, written out

Each slide below is the actual content. Copy it, don't paraphrase it — paraphrase is where numbers drift.

### Slide 1 — Title

```
7-Day BP Confirmation
The follow-up layer that Indian clinics and labs do not have

[Your name] · [City] · [Month Year]
₹25 lakh for 10–12%
```

One line, no jargon, no "revolutionising". The subtitle names the category you are creating.

### Slide 2 — The problem, in their language

```
A patient leaves with 150/95.
Someone says "check it at home."
Nobody knows if they did.

· 210 million Indian adults have hypertension
· 174 million are uncontrolled
· The clinic cannot tell you its own follow-up rate
```

| Uncontrolled | 173.9 million | [F] E2 |
| Clinic's own follow-up rate | **Unknown to them** | — |

**Say this out loud on the slide:** "You cannot currently answer the last line. That is the business." An investor who runs clinics will feel that sentence, because it is true of their own operation.

### Slide 3 — Why the devices made it worse

```
Wrist notifications now flag hypertension.
The FDA-cleared feature catches about 4 in 10 true cases
and tells the patient to see a doctor.

You are the doctor. Nobody built the step in between.
```

| Element | Value | Tag |
|---|---|---|
| Sensitivity of FDA-cleared consumer hypertension notification | 41.2% (n=2,229) | [F] E3 |
| Countries where wearable data reaches clinical workflow above 6% | **None** (2,222 physicians, 6 countries) | [F] E4 |

**The argument:** detection is becoming free and commoditised. The bottleneck is not sensors — it is the workflow between a reading and a decision. That workflow is the product.

### Slide 4 — What we do

```
7 days, 5 steps, one page.

Day 0    Enrol. Teach the technique. Printed card.
Day 1–7  Two readings a day. Reminder at the agreed time.
Day 8    One-page summary on the doctor's desk.
Monthly  One-page report: enrolled, completed, decisions.
Always   The doctor decides. We never do.
```

**Say the last line before they ask.** Every clinic owner in the room is wondering about liability. Naming it first converts a fear into a credibility signal.

Per site:   ₹30,000/month = ₹3.6 lakh/year
100 sites:  ₹3.6 crore/year
1,000 sites: ₹36 crore/year
```

| Layer | Number | Tag |
|---|---|---|

| Revenue per site per year | ₹3.6 lakh | [T] |
| Revenue at 100 sites | ₹3.6 crore | [T] |

**Then say the sentence that wins the room:** "I don't need to win the market. I need one hundred sites, and this city has four thousand." A clinic owner understands that arithmetic immediately; a market-size slide makes them suspicious.

### Slide 8 — Competition, honestly

| Who | What they do | Why they don't solve it |
|---|---|---|
| **The clinic's own staff** | Say "check at home" | No owner, no list, no follow-up. **This is the real competitor** |
| Consumer wearables | Detect and notify | 41.2% sensitivity; they hand the problem to the clinic [F] E3 |
| EMR / clinic software | Record readings | Record; do not chase patients |
| RPM platforms | Device + dashboard, sold to hospitals | Built for US billing; no India last-mile, no human follow-up |
| Diagnostic labs | Sell BP inside a package | One-off measurement, no 7-day confirmation |
| **Us** | Own the 7 days between reading and decision | Only if completion is measured and reported |

**"The clinic's own staff" in row one is the slide that makes sophisticated investors trust you.** Naming the real competitor instead of pretending the field is empty is the difference between a founder and a salesperson.

### Slide 10 — The 24-month plan

| Phase | Months | What is true at the end | Revenue run-rate |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Foundation** | 1–6 | 5 paying sites, a coordinator, a repeatable playbook | ₹1.0–1.5 L/month |
| **Repeatability** | 7–12 | 10 sites, product v1 live, second city tested | ₹2.5–3.0 L/month |
| **Durability** | 13–18 | 15 sites, break-even, study complete | ₹4.0 L/month |
| **Scale decision** | 19–24 | 20–25 sites, decision on a second condition or a licence | ₹5.0–6.0 L/month |

**The number that matters at month 24:** ₹60 lakh annual run-rate, break-even, and no need for another round. That is the plan, and it is deliberately unambitious. Ambition is what makes investors ask for a bigger market; a credible plan is what makes them write the cheque.

### Slide 12 — The ask, and the return

```
₹25 lakh for 10–12% at ₹2.0–2.5 crore post-money.

What it buys: 10 sites, break-even, and a validated product by month 18.

What you get:
  · 10–12% of a business targeting ₹3–5 crore revenue by year 5
  · Distributions from year 2 if the board agrees
  · First refusal on your region, or a licence for your own chain
  · A customer relationship, not just a shareholding
```

## 3. The honest risk slide (slide 13, or an appendix if you must keep to 12)

Do not omit this. A customer-investor who finds a risk you hid stops trusting every other number.

| # | Risk | Likelihood | What it does to the money | Mitigation |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Completion rate is structurally low** — patients will not do 7 days | Medium | Kills the model; the ₹25L is largely lost | Measured in the first 6 weeks, before this round is spent. Gate at month 12 |
| 2 | **Sites do not renew** — pilots are easy, retainers are not | Medium-high | Revenue plateaus at ₹1L/month; you return to a services business | Renewal is a gate before this round is raised at all |
| 3 | **You stay the bottleneck** — delivery does not transfer to a coordinator | Medium | Growth stops at 8–10 sites; valuation falls | Coordinator hired at month 7, not month 18 |
| 4 | **A big player bundles it** — a lab chain or an EMR vendor | Medium | Price pressure; slower sales | They are also potential acquirers and licensees. This risk has a floor |
| 5 | **Regulatory drift** — a feature is reclassified as a device | Low-medium | Product roadmap slows; ₹5–15 L of compliance cost | Deliberate scope: capture, remind, format, route. No clinical conclusions |
| 6 | **Key-person risk** — you get ill, or quit** | Low | The company stops | Documented playbook from month 9; advisor who can operate it |

**The line to say:** "The most likely way you lose money is not competition. It is that patients don't complete the series. We will know that in six weeks, for ₹15,000, before you invest."

That sentence is the strongest thing in the deck, because it shows you are optimising for the investor's downside, not your own upside.

## 4. The term sheet

```
INVESTMENT TERM SHEET (non-binding)

Investor:        [Name / entity]
Company:         [Entity], incorporated [date]
Instrument:      CCPS (compulsorily convertible preference shares)
Amount:          ₹25,00,000
Pre-money:       ₹1.75–2.25 crore
Post-money:      ₹2.00–2.50 crore
Investor stake:  10.0–12.5% on a fully diluted basis

VALUATION BASIS
  Stated openly: this valuation is based on N paying sites and ₹X
  monthly revenue, not on a discounted cash flow. If those numbers are
  not what the deck says, the valuation is open to renegotiation before
  signing.

LIQUIDATION PREFERENCE
  1× non-participating. On a sale, the Investor receives the greater of
  their money back or their pro-rata share.

ANTI-DILUTION
  Broad-based weighted average only. No full ratchet.

BOARD
  Observer seat, no vote. Board of 2: Founder + 1 independent.

RESERVED MATTERS (Investor consent required)
  · Issue of new shares or options outside an approved ESOP pool
  · Debt above ₹50 lakh
  · Sale, merger, or winding up of the Company
  · Change in the principal business line

  NOT reserved: hiring, salaries, individual spend below ₹10 lakh,
  pricing, vendor selection.

DIVIDEND
  From the first year in which the Company has distributable profits and
  the board recommends a distribution, subject to statutory requirements
  and retained working capital needs.

RIGHT OF FIRST REFUSAL
  Investor may license the product for their own network on standard
  commercial terms, negotiated separately. This is a commercial
  discussion, not part of the equity terms.

INFORMATION RIGHTS
  Monthly one-page operating report (the same report customers receive),
  quarterly financials, annual audited accounts.

FOUNDER VESTING
  None. The founder's shares are not subject to vesting.

ESOP
  8% pool, created post-money, so the dilution falls on all shareholders
  proportionally rather than entirely on the founder.

TRANSFER
  Tag-along for the Investor. No drag-along without a minimum valuation.

NOT LEGAL ADVICE
  To be documented by counsel for both parties. CCPS issuance requires
  board and shareholder approval and a valuation report under Indian law.
```

### 4.1 Why these terms, in one line each

| Term | Reason |
|---|---|
| CCPS, not equity | Standard in India, gives the investor a preference and the company a clean cap table |
| 1× non-participating | They get their money back or their share, not both. Participating preference at 2× is what makes founders get nothing in a modest sale |
| Broad-based weighted average | Protects them in a down round without transferring the company to them |
| Observer, not a board seat | They said they do not want the work. Give them the information, not the vote |
| Narrow reserved matters | The broader the list, the more the company stalls on routine decisions |
| No founder vesting | You built it before they arrived. Accept vesting only with credit for time served |
| ESOP post-money | Pre-money pools cost the founder ~1.5 points for every 10% created |

### 4.2 The valuation conversation, pre-answered

**"Why ₹2–2.5 crore for a business with ₹X revenue?"**

> "Because the valuation is not buying the revenue. It is buying a working pathway, a measured completion rate, and N clinics that have renewed. Rebuilding that takes 18 months and costs more than ₹25 lakh. If you would rather price it on revenue alone, we should not do this round — I would rather keep the equity and grow slower."

That is an honest answer, and it may end the conversation. It is better to end it than to accept a valuation you will resent for five years.

**"Why not raise from a VC?"**

> "Because you would not be here. A VC needs a ₹1,000 crore outcome; this business is built to reach ₹3–10 crore of revenue and pay its owners. I would rather have one investor who can introduce me to fifty lab owners than a fund that pushes me to open three cities before the first one works."

**"Why should I invest rather than just be a customer?"**

> "You should be a customer first regardless. Invest only if you want the region locked up and a share of the upside. If the pilot does not renew, I would rather you did not invest — and I will tell you that at the time."

## 5. The 24-month milestones and the gates that protect the money

| Month | Milestone | Gate if missed |
|---|---|---|
| 3 | Coordinator hired; 5 sites live | Do not hire further; founder delivers alone |
| 6 | ₹1.0–1.5 L/month; completion ≥55% | If completion <40%, stop software spend and redesign the pathway once |
| 9 | 8 sites; product v1 in use by 3 sites | If <5 sites, freeze hiring and extend runway |
| 12 | 10 sites; ₹2.5 L/month; first renewal cohort | **If <8 sites or <₹1.5 L/month, stop the hiring plan and return to a founder-only business.** Remaining capital covers 14 months instead of 12 |
| 15 | 13 sites; study protocol approved | If study stalls, delay the licence plan, not the service |
| 18 | 15 sites; **break-even**; study complete | If not break-even, cut to 10 sites and reprice |
| 21 | 18 sites; second city at 3 sites | If second city fails, do not open a third |
| 24 | 20–25 sites; ₹5–6 L/month; ₹60 L ARR run-rate | Decide: second condition, licence, or steady-state |

**The month-12 gate is the one to put in writing in the shareholders' agreement.** It is the mechanism that protects the investor's remaining capital and your credibility: a written, pre-agreed trigger for slowing down. Investors fund founders who have pre-committed to a stop rule.

## 6. What the money is not for

| Not funded | Why |
|---|---|
| Hardware | ₹5–30 crore and 24 months before revenue. Out of scope for this round and this decade |
| A consumer app | Patients will not download it for 7 days. WhatsApp is the app |
| Three cities at once | One city, then one more. Geographic spread before repeatability is how service businesses die |
| A large sales team | You are the sales team until 15 sites |
| A clinical-outcomes trial | That is a grant (`02-GRANT.md`) or a later round, not this money |
| Your full market salary | ₹25,000/month is in the budget and it is below market. Say it plainly |

Investors read the memo and skim the deck. Write the memo as if the deck does not exist.

```

1. WHAT IT IS
A 7-day home BP confirmation service for Indian clinics and labs. The

Home BP devices are cheap, WhatsApp is the default patient channel, and

You operate in this market. You can introduce us to the next ten sites,

₹25 lakh for 10–12% at ₹2.0–2.5 crore post. Buys 10 sites and break-even

evidence.

## 9. Why you cannot send this before a pilot renews

An investor asks one question first: **"Who pays you today?"**

| Your answer | What happens next |
|---|---|
| "We're pre-revenue but the market is huge" | Polite ending. No recovery, in this meeting or any later one |
| "One pilot, ₹15,000, it finished last month" | Interest, but the valuation conversation collapses to ₹50 lakh–1 crore |
| "Three sites, ₹45,000 collected, one renewed for ₹30,000/month" | The conversation you want. The valuation is defensible |

The difference between the second and third answer is **eight weeks of work**, not a better deck. Do the work.

**Send order:** `01-CLINIC-LAB.md` until you have a renewal. Then this document. In between, send `03-ADVISOR.md`, because a named clinical advisor changes how an investor reads slide 2.

## 10. Sources and assumptions

- [F] facts resolve in §16.2 of `Project-H-Market-Intelligence-Report.md`. Used here: E2 (India prevalence), E3 (41.2% sensitivity), E4 (6% workflow integration), E6 (India smartwatch ASP and volume), E7 (Oura margins and retention), E8 (Garmin margins).
- [T] figures are planning assumptions: price points, cost per patient, retainer bands, site counts, revenue and EBITDA, terminal multiple. None has been measured.
- [C] figures are the customer's own numbers and must be filled from your pilot data. **Do not estimate them.**
- The return illustration (§2, slide 12) is arithmetic on stated assumptions, not a forecast. A 3× revenue terminal multiple is a convention for small profitable service businesses in India, not a guarantee; multiples for services businesses are typically lower than for software.
- CCPS issuance, valuation reports, and dividend mechanics are governed by the Companies Act 2013 and require professional advice. This document is a commercial outline.
- **Verify before sending:** every [C] number, the current revenue, the renewal status, and the cap table. A stale deck is worse than no deck.
