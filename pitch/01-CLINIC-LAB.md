# 01 — Clinic / Lab Pitch

**Reader:** lab owner, clinic manager, physiotherapy or cardiac-rehab centre owner
**Ask:** ₹15,000 for a 6-week paid pilot, 30 patients
**Format:** 4 pages, printed. This document is the leave-behind, not a script.

## Page 2 — The six slides (if you get a screen)

Six slides maximum. A clinic meeting is 12 minutes; six slides is already generous.

| # | Headline (say this exactly) | Body | The number |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | **"You see high readings every day. Nobody follows them up."** | A patient leaves with 150/95. They are told to check at home. Whether they do is unknown to you, to them, and to anyone else | 0 — that's how many of your patients you can currently account for |
| 2 | **"This is not a small gap."** | Hypertension is the most common chronic condition in India, and most people who have it are not controlled | 210 million adults in India; 174 million uncontrolled [MR S12] |
| 3 | **"The devices made it worse, not better."** | Wrist notifications now flag people. The FDA-cleared hypertension feature catches only about 4 in 10 true cases, and then tells the patient to see a doctor. You are the doctor | 41.2% sensitivity [MR S13, S14] |
| 4 | **"So the missing step is confirmation — and it takes 7 days."** | Not a device, not an app. A protocol, a reminder sequence, and one page on your desk | 7 days, 2 readings/day |
| 5 | **"Your risk is capped at ₹7,500."** | ₹15,000 for 30 patients, half on signing. If fewer than 20 complete, you don't pay the rest | ₹500/patient at full price |
| 6 | **"In 6 weeks you'll know your own number."** | Completion rate, doctor-action rate, and how many follow-ups you were losing. You keep the report either way | 3 metrics, 1 page |

**Slide 6 is why this closes.** You are selling information about their own clinic that they cannot currently buy, delivered alongside a working process.

   All diagnosis and treatment decisions remain solely with the

   Patient data is processed on the Institution's behalf under its consent

7. SUCCESS CRITERIA (measured, reported, and shared)
   · Series completion rate (target ≥55%)
   · Summary delivered before consultation (target ≥90%)
   · Documented treatment decision following summary (target ≥50%)

**Negotiation notes (internal, do not send):**

- If they push on price, reduce patients: 20 patients for ₹12,000. Never reduce the per-patient rate.
- If they ask for free, offer **10 patients free, once**, and say explicitly it is a one-time demonstration, not a precedent. Then stop.
- If they want a longer pilot, that is a good sign but a cash-flow problem. Fixed 6 weeks, renewed monthly.
- If they ask who else uses it, say the truth: "You would be the first. That's why the price is ₹15,000 and the risk is mine."

## Page 4 — Objection sheet (internal, never leave behind)

| They say | They mean | You say |
|---|---|---|
| "We already tell patients to check at home." | There is no follow-up owner | "Who calls the ones who don't? That's the only part I'm asking to take." |
| "Our doctors won't read another report." | Format problem | "One page, three lines, on top of the file. If it takes more than 30 seconds, I'll change it or you don't pay." |
| "Patient data cannot leave our system." | Legitimate DPDP concern | "It doesn't. I work on your premises or your business number, under your consent, and you own the record. Clause 6 in the proposal says exactly that." |
| "Send me something, I'll review it." | Polite deferral | "I'll leave this page. Can I book 10 minutes on [specific day] to walk through the summary format? That's the only part that needs your judgement." |
| "What's your background? Are you a doctor?" | Trust | "No. I run the logistics; your doctor makes every clinical decision. That separation is why you don't need to worry about liability." |
| "₹15,000 is a lot for 30 patients." | Unclear return | "It's ₹500 a patient. One recovered follow-up consult plus one monitoring package is worth more than that in a year. And if 20 don't complete, you pay half." |
| "We tried a vendor before." | Prior failure | "What did they ask your staff to do? My version asks staff to hand over a card. Everything else is mine." |
| "Let's do it after the season / next quarter." | No urgency | "Understood. Can I run 10 patients free in the meantime so you have something concrete to look at?" — then only once |

## The 12-minute meeting script

| Minute | What you do |
|---|---|
| 0–2 | Ask: "When a patient leaves with a high reading, what happens next?" Let them talk. Do not correct anything |
| 2–4 | Ask: "How do you know how many come back?" **Wait through the silence.** This is the moment the sale happens |
| 4–6 | Show the sample summary. One page. "This is what the doctor gets" |
| 6–8 | Explain the 7 days in five sentences. No slides |
| 8–10 | Price and risk reversal: ₹15,000, half now, completion guarantee |
| 10–12 | Ask for a start date. Not "what do you think" — "shall we start on the 14th or the 21st?" |

**If they say yes:** sign on the spot, take the ₹7,500, and book the protocol session before you leave the building. A verbal yes decays within 72 hours.

**If they say no:** one question — "Is it the price, the process, or the timing?" Log the answer verbatim. Twenty nos with reasons is a better asset than one yes without one.

---

## What never to say

- "We're like Apple Health, but…" — invites a comparison you lose.
- "AI" or "algorithm" — there is no model. Say "protocol" and "summary."
- "We improve BP control" — you have no evidence of that and cannot get it in 6 weeks.
- "We'll integrate with your system" — there is nothing to integrate yet.
- "We're a startup" — irrelevant to them, and it invites a discount request.
